Q: So, other than purposefully misinterpreting the question and giving a silly answer, are there

A: How do we add humour into conversations? I'm coming back to a lot of the same things, which is
that your initial conversational tracks are going to be totally similar — and they already are.
People say the same things. If you're in college they want to know what major you have; if you're
in the workforce they want to know what you do for a living. So having BOTH an answer that is
true and reveals your values, AND a playful answer, to a lot of these different things. You can
answer: what are the three questions that you get asked the most? I literally went out and
split-tested these different things and would just answer different stuff at different times in
the nightclub or something like that. So that's one you can do, because those are just going to
be free gimmes. It's kind of like uncle jokes — the uncle's just prepared for you to say
something and he has a ready-made response.

In terms of more spontaneous conversational stuff, there are a lot of different types of humour.
The Russell Brand one is very fast, clever, callback, witty — that's tougher to implement. An
easy one is TO SAY THE OPPOSITE. And it sounds dumb, but if it's a super hot day and everyone's
complaining about how hot it is, and you go out and you're like "man, it's cold out here", I
swear to God you will get a chuckle from people. Because what humour is, in many cases, is just
when the unexpected occurs — it causes a chuckle. So it's not the funniest way to be funny; you
can be witty and clever and have interesting callbacks. But saying the opposite is a very easy
way to get laughs and chuckles and insert humour.

The other thing I recommend people do — because I do think this is more System One, just
automatic — is YOU CAN PRIME YOURSELF. How I did it wasn't by purposely working in "saying the
opposite" necessarily. It was by watching a lot of Chris Pratt before I would go out. I just
watched 10 minutes, and I'd watch some Step Brothers best-of clips, and put myself in that mode
of ridiculous non-sequitur statements. And I would just lift these lines straight out of movies
into contexts where they didn't make any sense, and that actually worked really well — because I
was enjoying it, I was referencing movies I had fun with. I was not taking all of the words that
Chris Pratt said, but his general demeanour was coming through. So: trying to make your fifth
best friend the comedian that you most enjoy and the vibe that you want to have, and just
watching them for 10–15 minutes before going into a social interaction, was actually the way that
I did it.
